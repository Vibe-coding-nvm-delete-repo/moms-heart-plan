How Many Steps, Exactly

Every extra 1,000 steps goes with about 15% fewer deaths (226,889 people). The "10,000 steps" rule came from a 1960s ad in Japan. It was never science.

Questions for Her Doctor

Print this page and bring it. These questions turn the plan into the right plan for her.

  • What is her calcium score from the scan? Ask for the number and the report.
  • Given that score, should she take a statin, bempedoic acid, or neither? (The ledger below shows why the calcium score decides this.)
  • What is her blood pressure goal? Aiming near 130 or less cut deaths and events in two big trials (SPRINT, STEP).
  • Has she ever had her Lp(a) checked? It is a one time blood test and it runs in families.
  • Can we test ApoB, hsCRP, A1c, vitamin D level, and kidney function?
  • Is she due for a mammogram, colon test, bone scan, or lung scan (if she ever smoked)?
  • Should she take aspirin? The default answer at her age is no, unless she had a heart attack or a stent.
  • Are her current supplements safe with her medicines? Bring the whole bag.

Myths That Waste Money or Cause Harm

You need 10,000 steps.

False. That number came from a 1960s ad for a Japanese pedometer. The benefit maxes out at 6,000 to 8,000 steps for people over 60.

High good cholesterol cancels out high bad cholesterol.

False. HDL does not wash LDL out of arteries. A "good ratio" with a high LDL is still a high LDL. Big studies of HDL raising drugs found no saved lives.

My calcium score went up, so the treatment failed.

Not so fast. Statins and exercise can make plaque denser, and denser plaque is more stable. Doctors care about plaque density, not just the number.

No flush niacin is a gentler version.

False. The flushing kind is the only kind that works. The no flush pills do nothing for cholesterol.

Older people should take a daily aspirin just in case.

False. A trial of 19,114 adults 70 and up found more bleeding and more deaths, with no benefit. Only use it if a doctor prescribes it after a heart attack, stroke, or stent.

Big calcium pills protect her bones.

Not the best way. Food calcium (yogurt, greens), plus D3, K2, and strength work, is safer for the heart.

The Statin Question, Both Sides Honestly

Aspirin: the case against is clear for her

ASPREE trial (19,114 adults 70 and up, 65 and up for some US groups): no benefit on the main goal, major bleeding up 38%, death up 14%. Two other big trials agreed. Skip it unless it is prescribed after a heart event.

What this means for Mom

The no benefit studies are realThey studied people with fairly clean arteries. When few people are having events, few people can be saved.
Mom has calcium on her scansThat moves her out of the "clean arteries" group. Her event risk is several times higher, so a 22% relative cut means more.
The decision ruleFind her calcium score. Score 100 or more: the case for treatment is strong. Score 0 to 99 with low risk: skipping is a fair, evidence based choice.
If she cannot tolerate a statinBempedoic acid proved itself in a trial of 13,970 statin intolerant patients (Nissen 2023: events down 13%, LDL down 21%).
The fair summary: walking and food are the heroes of this plan. Aspirin as prevention is a proven bad idea at her age. The statin question is settled by her calcium score, not by opinion.

Foods for Everyday Meals

Start with beans, oats or barley, soy, nuts, vegetables, fruit, and olive oil. Add fish if you eat it. This list joins Mediterranean meals with the cholesterol-focused Portfolio pattern. You do not need every food on this list.

Two editorial scores, each out of 10: Health weighs fiber, useful nutrients, unsaturated fats, and how a food can replace a less helpful choice. Diet fit weighs how directly it supports these two eating patterns. A 10 is an especially useful everyday choice, not a measured benefit for one food. These are not clinical scores, a promise to lower LDL or heart risk, or personal medical approval. A 7 can still be a good choice. Preparation and portion matter.

Choose unsalted or lower-sodium versions when you can. Check allergies, medicine and kidney-related diet rules with your care team. If you take warfarin, keep vitamin K intake steady rather than avoiding greens on your own. Psyllium powder is a separate fiber supplement, not a vegetable or one of the groceries below.

What the studies tested: A full Portfolio diet has nuts, soy or other plant protein, sticky fiber from foods such as oats and barley, and plant sterols. A 6-month trial found about 13% lower LDL with that pattern, compared with about 3% with standard diet advice. A smaller, intensive 4-week trial found 29.6% lower LDL; that is not a promise for everyday meals or a three-item pill stack. Mediterranean eating emphasizes plant foods, olive oil and fish. Neither trial measured a 1-to-10 score for the individual foods above.

In a study of 25,315 women followed for about 25 years, higher Mediterranean diet scores were linked to lower death risk, not proved to cause it. The highest versus lowest group had a hazard ratio of 0.77 after initial adjustment and 0.89 after adjustment for lifestyle factors. In the corrected PREDIMED trial, heart events were 3.8% with extra olive oil, 3.4% with nuts and 4.4% in the control group over a median 4.8 years. The relative hazard ratios were 0.69 and 0.72; the absolute differences were 0.6 and 1.0 percentage points. The correction disclosed problems with the original random assignment.

Read the food-pattern studies
  • Portfolio diet, 6-month trial (Jenkins 2011): 351 people; full diet groups lowered LDL by 13.1% to 13.8%, versus 3.0% with standard advice.
  • Intensive Portfolio diet (Jenkins 2005): 34-person, 4-week crossover; LDL was 29.6% lower during the intensive diet phase.
  • Mediterranean diet and women (JAMA 2024): an observational association across 25,315 women, not a randomized diet result.
  • Corrected PREDIMED trial (NEJM 2018): heart-event rates and the random assignment caveat.

Fish Oil: EPA vs DHA

The answer is EPA

Why EPAThe plaque shrinking trial (EVAPORATE) and the big outcomes trial (REDUCE-IT) used pure prescription EPA. Japan's big EPA trial (18,645 people) cut heart events 19%.
The DHA problemDHA heavy formulas can raise LDL by 3 to 5%. The failed omega-3 trial (STRENGTH) used an EPA plus DHA blend.
What to buyAny fish oil where EPA is at least double DHA on the label. Take it with a meal.
DosesGeneral: 1 to 2 g of EPA a day. If her triglycerides are 135 or higher on a statin, ask the doctor for the 4 g prescription version (events down 25%, plaque down 17%).
Max safe3 g a day of total EPA plus DHA without a doctor. The audit note: in REDUCE-IT the AFib rise (3.1% vs 2.1%) was real and significant; the bleeding difference (2.7% vs 2.1%) was not proven. More is still not better.

Vitamin K2: Buy MK-7

The answer is MK-7

The right oneMK-7, made by fermenting natto. The coronary calcium trials used this form. It lasts about 3 days in the body, so one small pill a day works.
Why not the othersMK-4 fades in hours and needs huge doses. K1, from greens, has no calcium trial results.
Dose and rules180 to 360 mcg with the fattiest meal of the day. No known toxic limit, but never with warfarin.
Honest limitsFrom the audit: the 2026 trial was small (180 people) and measured calcium growth, not heart attacks. DANCODE tested K2 PLUS D3, so K2's solo share is unclear. And a 2 year trial in patients with heart VALVE calcium found no benefit. Artery calcium: yes. Valve calcium: unproven.

Garlic: Buy Aged Garlic Extract

The answer is Aged Garlic Extract

The right oneAged Garlic Extract (the Kyolic type), aged in alcohol for about 20 months. It is standardized to a compound called SAC. The calcium trials used this exact form.
The wrong onesRaw garlic, garlic oil, and "high allicin" pills. Different chemistry, no trial results.
Dose1,200 mg a day (two 600 mg pills) with meals. Up to 2,400 mg has been studied and is safe.

Blood Pressure: The Forgotten Lever

Have her pressure checked at home too: sit 5 minutes, feet flat, arm supported, no talking. Two readings, morning and evening, for a week. Bring the notes to the doctor.

Outside the Box: The Underground List, Graded Honestly

This pass went looking where mainstream reviews do not go: X threads, Reddit stacks (r/Supplements, biohacker forums), holistic clinics, and repurposed cheap drugs. Every candidate then got graded against whatever real human evidence exists. Fair warning about bias: drug companies slant studies, and so does the $170 billion supplement industry. We grade the evidence, not the source.

Power score: how much it moves her risk. No mark = randomized trials with real outcomes. s = imaging, blood, or secondary results. o = observational. 7-8 strong 5-6 solid 3-4 small 1-2 tiny
TreatmentPowerWhat the evidence says
Sauna, 4-7 sessions a week7oThe Finnish cohort (2,315 men, 20+ years) found about 50% lower cardiovascular death at 4-7 sessions a week vs once a week, with sudden cardiac death also much lower. A second study confirmed it in men and women. Observational, but biologically real: heat works like mild cardio (heart rate up, blood pressure down, heat shock proteins protect cells). Mom rules: start at 2-3 sessions of 10-15 minutes at moderate heat, hydrate well, exit if dizzy. If her heart valve is narrowed (aortic stenosis), get doctor clearance first, because heat widens vessels while a narrowed valve cannot compensate.
B vitamins (B6 + B12 + folate)5The underground "homocysteine theory" half won its trial. HOPE-2 (5,522 patients with vascular disease, randomized): stroke down 25%, with less stroke disability too. Heart attacks and deaths: no change. So it is a stroke-specific helper, cheap and safe. Worth asking her doctor about, since her arteries are already diseased.
Gum disease treatment (periodontal therapy)4sThe one almost nobody thinks of. Gum disease drives body-wide inflammation, and the American Heart Association links it to artery disease. Controlled reviews show treating it lowers CRP and other heart-risk blood markers. No outcome trial yet, but it costs a dental cleaning and has zero downside. Flossing is cardiovascular care.
Beetroot juice (dietary nitrate)4s22 randomized trials: systolic blood pressure down about 4 points, better with regular use. Works through nitric oxide, the same pathway exercise uses, and improves walking capacity in older adults. A glass a day or concentrated shots. Real, modest, cheap.
Time-restricted eating3sEating within a daily window (for example 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.) improves blood pressure, insulin, and weight in small trials. This is the insulin-resistance angle from the Lustig clip your mom's plan already leans on. Small proof, but the direction is right and it is free. For a 67-year-old: keep protein high and do not skip meals if she is underweight.
EECP (pneumatic cuffs on the legs)3Hospital sessions of inflated leg cuffs that push blood back to the heart. FDA cleared for chest pain that drugs cannot fix. It improves angina symptoms and exercise tolerance in real trials. It does not reverse plaque, but for symptoms it is a legitimate non-drug option doctors rarely mention.
Colchicine 0.5 mg (the anti-pharma drug)8Already on the mortality tab, but it belongs here: a generic gout medicine that costs pennies, has no patent, and cut heart events 25% in pooled trials. The least profitable heart drug ever proven. If she has established disease plus high inflammation, it belongs on the doctor-questions list.
Lumbrokinase (earthworm enzyme)2sBig in Chinese medicine and biohacker forums. A 2024 meta-analysis in stroke patients found "promise" but low-to-moderate quality evidence and a role "not well established." No human plaque data at all. Also carries bleeding risk with blood thinners. Not for mom.
Pomegranate juice2sOne small Israeli trial (about 19 people) suggested slower carotid thickening. Never replicated, and a glass of juice is a sugar bomb. Eat the seeds if she likes them; skip it as therapy.
Dan shen and notoginseng (Chinese herbs)2sWidely prescribed in China for heart disease. Trials are small, low quality, and products vary wildly. Quality control is the real danger. Not for mom.
NMN / NR (NAD boosters)2sThe longevity-circuit favorite. Human trials so far show tiny or no effects on blood pressure or arteries. Hype is years ahead of the data, and prices are high.
EDTA chelation therapy1The flagship underground treatment, and it got a fair test. The NIH funded it twice. TACT (2013) hinted at benefit. TACT2 (JAMA 2024, 1,000 diabetic heart attack survivors): events 35.6% vs 35.7%, no difference. It cut blood lead 61% and still saved no one. The expensive infusion version is now dead as a heart treatment. Oral chelation pills are even weaker.
Serrapeptase1The silk-worm enzyme heavily pushed on social media. There are zero published human studies showing it dissolves artery plaque. One honest reviewer called the plaque claim "marketing fiction." Skip.
Vitamin C + lysine (Pauling therapy)1Linus Pauling's 1990s theory. Large vitamin C trials since then found no heart benefit. Theory did not survive contact with data.
Hydrogen water1Tiny short trials on blood pressure and oxidation markers. Nothing solid. Skip.
The pattern worth noticing: what the forums push hardest (chelation, serrapeptase, nattokinase) keeps failing real trials, while the unglamorous stuff nobody hypes (heat exposure, B vitamins, teeth cleaning, beets, walking) keeps showing real signals. The underground winners are boring. The dramatic cures are marketing.
What Reddit and X actually recommend most (K2, nattokinase, berberine, serrapeptase, EDTA, high-dose vitamin C): of those six, two earned a place on this page (K2 at 6, berberine at 4) and four failed their evidence check. Community enthusiasm predicts popularity, not truth.

NiacinOnly the flushing kind works. Above 500 mg a day, a doctor should watch blood sugar, liver, and gout. One 81 mg aspirin 30 minutes before stops most flushing.

Vitamin K2Never with warfarin. Otherwise very safe.
